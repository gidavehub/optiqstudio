# OPTIQ DOCUMENTARY — GOLD-STANDARD SCENE PROMPT EXEMPLAR
This is the house benchmark for a dense (~1,500-word) SILENT documentary scene prompt: every worker, every surface, every object authored, nobody speaking, nobody looking at the lens, no text on screen. Match this density and this block order. Do NOT copy its subject, its place or its people.

### DOCUMENTARY 01 · SCENE 4 — THE LOADING RAMP · 10s

```
STYLE: Observational documentary, close and patient. Natural available light only, honest colour, fine film grain, no gloss and no grade-for-effect. The camera is a WITNESS, not a participant: it does not lead the action, it catches up with it. Hand-held with weight, never shaky-for-style. Shallow depth of field on the hands, deeper on the wide. COMPLETELY PHOTOREALISTIC - genuine documentary footage, not CGI, not a commercial. No character looks into the lens, nobody poses, nobody smiles for the camera, no slow motion on people. The footage is SILENT OF SPEECH; the film's narration is recorded separately and laid over the finished cut. No on-screen text of any kind.

=== ABSOLUTE RULES (follow strictly) ===
- NO MUSIC of any kind. No soundtrack, no melody, no instrumental bed, no humming or singing, no music from any radio, phone or speaker in the scene.
- NO SPEECH of any kind. Nobody speaks. No lips move in speech - not talking, not mouthing, not mid-sentence. No dialogue, no conversation, no shouted instruction. This clip contains no audible words.
- NO ON-SCREEN TEXT of any kind. No captions, subtitles, lower thirds, names, dates, numbers, charts or title cards. No readable writing on crates, walls or clothing.
- NOBODY LOOKS AT THE LENS. No posing, no acknowledgement of being filmed.

THE SETTING - A CONCRETE LOADING RAMP BEHIND A GAMBIAN FISH LANDING SITE, 6:40am: a low poured-concrete ramp, cracked across its width in two places and patched with a darker grey cement that never quite matched, running down from a corrugated-zinc shed to the packed-earth turning circle where a flatbed truck is backed up. The concrete is permanently wet, running with meltwater and fish-water that has cut two shallow channels along the slope and pools at the bottom in a flat brown puddle skinned with scales. Along the left edge: a stack of thirty-odd blue plastic fish crates, some cracked at the corner, all of them stained down one side, stacked five high and leaning. Along the right: a hand-painted galvanised tub of coarse ice going grey at the top, a bent aluminium shovel standing in it, three rolls of clear plastic sheeting, a coil of blue polypropylene rope, and a wooden pallet with one slat missing. The zinc shed wall behind is rusted through at the base in a ragged brown line, its lower half splashed pale from years of hosing. A single bare bulb on a hooked flex still burns above the shed door, pointless now in the daylight. Beyond the truck: the flat grey water of the landing site, four painted pirogues nosed onto the sand, and a low bank of morning haze that has not lifted yet. Everything is wet. Nothing here is new; everything is mended.

THE PEOPLE IN FRAME - all of them Black Gambian / Black West African, all of them working, none of them speaking, none of them aware of the camera:
- THE LOADER ON THE RAMP: a Black Gambian man in his early forties, deep umber-brown skin with cool undertones, a completely clean-shaven bald head, a thick full black beard grown long but clean, tall and broad and powerfully built, a strong square jaw with a visible chin cleft. He wears a faded ORANGE polo gone almost salmon at the shoulders, sleeves pushed up over the elbows, GREEN plastic waders folded down to the waist, and split black rubber boots. His forearms are wet to the elbow.
- THE CATCHER ON THE TRUCK BED: a Black Gambian man in his late teens, around eighteen, medium warm-brown skin with a soft glow, short black hair in a low tidy fade with a clean hairline, wiry and athletic with visible muscle definition, one ear noticeably more prominent than the other. He wears a sleeveless GREY vest, YELLOW rubber gloves too big for him, and BLUE tracksuit bottoms rolled to mid-calf.
- THE ICE WOMAN AT THE TUB: a Black Gambian woman in her fifties, very deep blue-black skin with a soft matte finish, black hair wrapped in a tall printed RED-AND-WHITE headwrap with no hair visible, short and stocky and broad through the shoulders, faint tribal facial marks on both cheeks. Wax-print wrapper in GREEN and OCHRE with a PLASTIC APRON tied over it, and flip-flops in standing water.
- BACKGROUND, four people, none of them in focus: a very tall lean man in his late twenties with shoulder-length black locs worn loose, light copper-brown skin, hauling a crate up the far side of the ramp; a woman in her mid-thirties with golden-brown skin and chunky black cornrows into a low bun, squatting beside the puddle sorting small fish into a basin by size; a young man of about nineteen, rich dark-brown skin and a very short buzz cut, standing on the pallet holding a length of rope somebody has handed him; an elder in his late sixties, mid-toned chestnut-brown skin and a greying salt-and-pepper beard, seated on an upturned crate at the shed door with a tally book on his knee, not writing.

[0-2.0s] The loader's hands come into frame low and take a full crate off the top of the stack - the crate comes away wet, and a broad sheet of meltwater sluices off its base onto the concrete and runs down the slope past his boots. He turns with it against his hip in one motion, no pause.

[2.0-4.0s] He swings it up and OUT toward the truck bed. The catcher takes it at chest height, absorbs the weight with a visible dip of the knees, and the crate's contents shift audibly inside it. He pivots and slots it into the second row, hard, against the one already there.

[4.0-6.0s] HARD CUT to the ice tub: the ice woman drives the bent aluminium shovel down into the coarse ice, twists it to break the crust, and lifts a shovel-load clear. Ice water runs off the blade in a rope. She swings it over the nearest crate and tips - the ice goes in in one heavy slab-fall, spreading over the fish, and three or four pieces bounce out onto the concrete and skitter away down the slope.

[6.0-8.0s] HARD CUT back to the ramp, wider now: the loader is already two crates further on, and the stack behind him is visibly shorter than it was. Behind him the tall man with the locs reaches the top of the ramp and sets his crate down on the pallet with a knock; the young man with the rope steps off the pallet to make room for it without being told.

[8.0-10.0s] The catcher shoves the last crate of the row flush with his gloved palm, straightens, and drags the plastic sheeting across the loaded rows in one pull - it snaps taut and settles. Behind him the truck's suspension has dropped visibly on its springs, sitting lower than it did at the start of the shot. Hold two beats on the loaded bed and the running water.

THE SILENCE DECLARATION: No character speaks at any point in this clip. No lips move in speech, nobody mouths words, nobody is mid-sentence, nobody calls out and there is no conversation of any kind. The clip contains no audible words. Every instruction between these workers passes through hands, eyes and the work itself. The film's narration is recorded separately and laid over the finished cut.

CAMERA: Hand-held, at working height, close to the hands. Opens TIGHT on the crate and the loader's forearms - roughly 35mm, shallow, the stack behind him falling out of focus. Follows the crate across to the truck in one unbroken move rather than cutting on the handover, so the weight reads. HARD CUT to a tighter angle on the shovel and the ice, low, almost at tub height. HARD CUT back to a WIDER frame from the bottom of the ramp, taking in the slope, the stack, the truck and the background workers in one. The camera arrives slightly late on each beat, the way a real operator does. No crane, no drone, no dolly, no slow motion. Faces are caught in passing and never held; nobody is framed as a portrait.

LIGHTING: Flat, cool early-morning daylight under haze - no sun disc, no shadows with hard edges, no golden hour. The wet concrete throws back a dull sheet of skylight that fills the underside of faces and arms. The bare bulb over the shed door adds one small warm point in the top-left of the wide frame and lights nothing. Ice reads bright, water reads grey-brown, skin reads true and unglamorous. Nothing here is lit for effect.

COLOR: Muted, cool and real - grey concrete, grey water, grey haze, rusted brown zinc, the washed blues of the crates, the orange polo and the green waders standing out because they are the only saturated things in the frame. Fish silver. Ice white going grey. NOT a teal-and-orange grade, not warmed up, not lifted. It looks like the morning it was.

SOUND (diegetic only; NO MUSIC of any kind - no soundtrack, no melody, no instrumental bed, no humming or singing, no music from any radio, phone or speaker in the scene; and NO SPEECH of any kind - no dialogue, no voices, no audible words, nobody's lips moving in speech, because the narration is recorded separately): [LOCKED SOUND SPEC PASTED VERBATIM HERE - the film's unscored, wordless bed: the flat open air of the landing site, water running continuously on concrete, the low idle of a truck engine somewhere off to the right, gulls at distance, no music and no voices anywhere in it, recorded close and dry with no reverb tail and no sweetening.] Over that bed, this scene's own events: the suck and slap of a wet crate lifting off a stack; a broad sheet of water hitting concrete and running; boots on wet ground; the dull knock of plastic on plastic as a crate seats; the grunt of effort as weight is taken (breath, not words); the shovel's blade grinding into coarse ice and the crack of the crust breaking; the heavy hiss-and-thud of ice going in; loose pieces skittering away down the slope; plastic sheeting snapping taut; the truck's springs settling.

Hyper-realistic, observational, documentary. The SAME concrete ramp, the SAME wet grey morning: crates coming off a leaning stack and going onto a truck bed hand to hand, ice broken out of a tub and tipped in, the stack visibly shorter and the truck visibly lower by the end of the shot. Completely photorealistic - genuine documentary footage, not a commercial. Flat cool hazy daylight, muted honest colour, hand-held camera at working height that arrives late on each beat. NOBODY SPEAKS and no lips move in speech; NO MUSIC of any kind; NO on-screen text of any kind; nobody looks at the lens.
```
